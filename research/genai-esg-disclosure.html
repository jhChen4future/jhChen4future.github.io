GenAI, ESG Disclosure, and Investor Decisions

What happens when managers know that investors may use GenAI to read ESG reports? This exploratory experiment follows both sides of the information chain: how managers adapt disclosure and how AI assistance shapes investor judgment.

01 Question

Does an AI reader change the information it receives?

Rather than treating GenAI only as an investor tool, the study places it inside a manager-AI-investor system. Managers choose the text and format complexity of an ESG report while anticipating different probabilities that an investor will use AI; investors then evaluate the adapted report with or without AI assistance.

83valid participants, with analysis samples varying by task and missingness
9laboratory sessions conducted at Zhejiang University in March 2026
3incentivized business-decision rounds with rematching
02 Findings

Exploratory patterns in disclosure and judgment.

01

Managers adapted reports to expected AI use.

Between the 0% and 100% AI-use scenarios, mean chosen text complexity increased by about 41.8% and mean format complexity by about 41.0%. Within this experimental setting, managers responded to an anticipated AI reader rather than treating the audience as fixed.

02

AI assistance coincided with higher ESG ratings.

Mean ESG ratings were 79.83 in the AI-assisted condition and 73.13 in the human-only condition (p = .0018). The controlled estimate was approximately 6.28 points, suggesting that assistance did not necessarily move judgments closer to the reference scores used in the experiment.

03

Comprehension fell without a matching loss of confidence.

AI-assisted investors answered about 0.69 fewer report-comprehension questions correctly in the reported regression, while decision confidence was not statistically distinguishable across conditions. This gap is consistent with an illusion of understanding, but the evidence remains exploratory.

04

The two sides may reinforce one another.

The thesis describes a possible supply-side response, or machine greenwashing, alongside demand-side reliance on AI-generated interpretation. The combined pattern motivates studying GenAI as part of an endogenous disclosure system rather than as a neutral layer added after a report is written.

03 Interpretation

A two-sided information problem.

The manager-side pattern is consistent with strategic adaptation to beliefs about what an AI can detect. On the investor side, greater reliance on AI and a reported feeling of having fully understood the company offer plausible pathways from assistance to higher ratings and weaker independent comprehension.

These mechanisms are not evidence that every manager will manipulate an AI reader or that AI assistance generally harms investment decisions. They instead identify a feedback loop worth testing: once information providers anticipate machine interpretation, both the message and the human response to it may change.

04 Scope

What the archive can and cannot support.

The evidence comes from a modest student sample at one laboratory, shortened ESG reports, simplified disclosure choices, and a single AI configuration. The sample met the study's minimum planned power requirement, but it does not support broad claims about professional managers, investors, other models, or real capital markets.

The public repository contains the Chinese thesis, sanitized oTree code, experimental stimuli, and English technical guides. Participant-level data and analysis scripts are not yet public, so the archive supports inspection and interface reconstruction rather than complete replication.
